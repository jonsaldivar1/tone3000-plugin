T3KJ

Manual: loading tones, the block card, scenes and footswitches, presets, MIDI with the Quad Cortex, and automating in REAPER.

T3KJ is Jon's build of the free, open-source TONE3000 plugin: NAM captures and impulse responses in a signal chain, plus My Gear, scenes, footswitches, a split channel strip, skins and cover art.

1Start here

T3KJ loads NAM captures (amps, pedals, full rigs) and IRs (cabinets, rooms) as blocks in a signal chain. A NAM capture is a snapshot of real gear at the knob settings it was captured with: to change the amp's knobs, load another capture (a "tone" can hold several, one per setting) or shape it with the block EQ and the faceplate.

  1. Run T3KJ-v…-windows-x64.exe: standalone app, VST3 and CLAP. It installs next to the official TONE3000 plugin without touching it. Windows may warn the first time: More info → Run anyway.
  2. REAPER: Options → Preferences → Plug-ins → VST → Re-scan, then add T3KJ to your guitar track, after dJONt if you use it.
  3. Standalone: open T3KJ from the Start menu, then ☰ → Settings for your audio interface (ASIO for the lowest latency), inputs, outputs and MIDI inputs.
  4. Click + in the chain to load a tone, or drag a .nam / .wav file (or a folder) onto it.
Hover anything. The bar at the bottom explains every control as you point at it (turn it back on in Settings if you hid it). Most controls also have a right-click (advanced decks, menus).

What T3KJ adds to the stock TONE3000 plugin

3Loading tones

My Gear: Pinned, Recent and your Library. Browse TONE3000 is at the bottom.

Right-click a block: Copy, Load File, Load Folder, Footswitch (Set Art for local tones).

My Gear (your own files)

Click + (or a block's swap button) and My Gear opens first. Change folder points it at the folder where you keep captures (subfolders included, three levels deep). Every folder that holds .nam or IR .wav files shows up as one tone with all its models; loose files show on their own. Pin favourites with the bookmark; Recent fills itself. Browse TONE3000 switches to the online search. (Settings → Open My Gear First turns this off.)

TONE3000 (online)

Sign in once (☰ → Login: a browser page, or a code on your phone). Search by title, gear, tags or creator; filter by gear type, format (NAM / IR), makes, creators, verified creators or calibrated; or open your Profile for Recently used, Favorites and your own uploads. Pick a tone and it lands in the slot.

Files and folders

Drag a .nam (A2), an IR .wav, or a folder of them onto a + slot, or right-click → Load File / Load Folder. No account needed. A folder becomes one block with every model inside it.

Do thisTo
Click a blockOpen its card (levels, EQ, model).
Drag a blockMove it in the chain. Alt-drag duplicates it.
Right-click → Copy, then right-click a + → PasteA copy with every setting.
Swap (in the card)Replace the tone, keep the slot.
Right-click → Set ArtYour own JPG / PNG as the tile's art (local tones; saved next to the files).

4The block card

A capture's card: the model picker (‹ AC30-6 TB Brl 3 › and 1/6) switches between the models in this tone.

ControlWhat it does
PowerBypass this block (top left of the card, or a footswitch / scene).
In / OutLevel into and out of the block, ±24 dB. Out comes after the Mix, so it moves the whole blend.
MixDry against wet: lower blends the block's input back in (handy on IRs and reverb-like captures).
Model picker‹ › steps through the captures in this tone (each is the gear at different settings); the folder icon lists them.
EQA 6-band EQ on the wet signal. PRE puts it before the capture (changes how it distorts), off = after. Sliders view for gains, Curve view to drag bands (scroll = width, Alt-click = reset). An outlined EQ button means it's shaping the sound.
Info (i)The tone's description, makes and tags, plus your own label and notes (local tones keep them in a tk3j.json beside the files).
NormalizeLevels the block's loudness so captures sit at a similar volume (Settings → Per-Block Normalization shows it).
NAM sizeLITE saves CPU, FULL is the most accurate (Settings → Per-Block NAM Size shows it).

Calibration: captures made with calibration data set their own input level from your interface's dBu (Settings → Calibration). Uncalibrated ones use Normalize instead. If you're not sure, leave both at their defaults.

5The faceplate

The bottom row works on the whole chain. Its knobs (all but Pitch) are also part of each scene (section 8).

ControlWhat it does
InputLevel into the chain. With a stereo input, its menu picks how the two channels enter: Stereo SUM, Stereo (channel 1 → Left chain, 2 → Right), Dual Mono (L and R each run the chain; twice the CPU), Left or Right only.
GateNoise gate threshold (−100 to 0 dB) and power. Right-click for Release (5–500 ms), Hold (0–200 ms) and Range (20–80 dB; 80 = full mute).
Pitch ShiftTransposes the DI before the amp, ±24 semitones: −2 plays drop D on a standard-tuned guitar. Off by default and hidden until Settings → Show Pitch Shift. On adds latency (11–31 ms, reported to REAPER). Right-click: STEP (whole semitones; off = smooth whammy sweep), Tonality (highs above it stay unshifted, keeps the pick natural), Buffer (shorter = less latency, longer = cleaner lows). Pitch is not per scene.
Bass / Middle / TrebleGlobal tone stack after the chain: ±20 dB at 150 Hz, ±15 dB at 425 Hz, ±10 dB at 1.8 kHz; 5 = flat. Its power switch bypasses it.
SpreadMono chain only: turns it into a wide double-tracked stereo sound with a short, wobbling delay on one side. Right-click for Wobble, Crossover (keeps the lows mono) and Diffuse. The mono-safety light warns if it would cancel on a mono sum.
OutputMaster level, ±24 dB.

Gate's advanced deck (right-click the Gate).

Pitch Shift's deck: STEP, Tonality, Buffer.

6Stereo, two chains and the split strip

Stereo mode with the Right chain branching off the Left one after the amp.

ControlWhat it does
StereoThe header switch: a Left and a Right chain, each with its own blocks. Mono runs only the Left chain to both outputs.
BranchClick the gap after a block: the other chain starts from that point (one amp into two cabs, say). Click the branch point to make the chains independent again.
Pan L / R, LinkWhere each chain sits; Link mirrors them.
Solo, ØHear one chain alone; flip a chain's polarity if the two sound hollow together.
Balance, AutoTrims the chains against each other (±12 dB). Auto: play for about 2 s and it matches their levels.
Align, AutoDelays one chain up to 24 ms when captures carry different latency. Auto runs a half-second sweep and also fixes flipped polarity.
SwapExchange the Left and Right chains.
Split stripWith two lanes running (stereo or dual mono), the lane buttons split the faceplate: lane 2 gets its own Input, Gate, tone stack and Output. Click lane 1 / lane 2 to choose which one the knobs edit.

7Presets

A preset is the whole chain (every block, its model and settings, with the captures embedded so it loads offline), the faceplate, and all of its scenes. It leaves out what belongs to your rig: calibration, oversampling, solo, input mode, the MIDI map and the window size.

ToDo this
SaveDisk icon, type a name. The same name overwrites: that's how you edit a preset.
Start from scratch+ (New) clears the chain and resets every control. Save first if you want to keep what you had.
Browse / rename / deleteClick the preset name.
ReorderDrag presets in the browser. Prev / Next and MIDI follow the order.
See PC numbersTurn on MIDI PC in the browser: each preset shows the Program Change that loads it.

Presets are plain files: Settings → Presets → Open presets folder. Copy them to back up or move to another computer.

8Scenes and footswitches

Hovering a scene marks the blocks it would turn on or off.

A scene is a version of the preset: for every block its power, In, Out, Mix and EQ, plus the faceplate (Input, Gate, Bass / Middle / Treble, Spread, Output, Balance). Not in scenes: Pitch (tuning stays put) and which capture a block has loaded (load the amps you want as separate blocks and turn them on per scene, like the "Amp Testing" setup). There's no save step: whatever you change in a scene is what that scene keeps, and scenes save with the preset.

ToDo this
SwitchClick a scene. Switching is instant: no capture reloads.
Add+ after the last scene, up to 8 (A to H). It starts as a copy of the one you're in.
NameDouble-click it (or right-click → Rename).
DeleteRight-click → Delete. Later scenes move up a letter. Undo brings it back.

Footswitches 1 to 8

Right-click a block → Footswitch… and pick 1 to 8. Every block on the same switch turns on and off together, and the switch shows as a chip next to the scenes (click it to stomp). Use footswitches for things you kick in during a scene (a boost, a delay); use scenes for whole changes.

9MIDI and the Quad Cortex

☰ → Settings → MIDI Mapping: Choose a control, then move the knob / press the switch on your controller (or type its CC). Mappings save with the plugin and in your REAPER project. In the standalone app, tick your controller under MIDI inputs first.

TargetHow it responds
Program ChangeNo mapping needed: PC n loads the preset at position n in the list (PC 0 = the first).
Scene (value 0–7 = A–H)The CC's value picks the scene: 0 = A, 1 = B… Values past the last scene are ignored. Reliable for automation: the value always says which scene.
Footswitch 1–8Each press flips the switch (on ↔ off). A momentary QC switch (127 then 0) flips once per stomp.
Block N PowerFlips that block on / off per press.
Knobs and power switchesFollow the CC value (an expression pedal on Pitch makes a whammy).
Previous / Next PresetStep through the list.
One footswitch, three devices. On the Quad Cortex, changing presets sends a Program Change: T3KJ and dJONt each load their preset at that number, so keep presets in the same order in both. For scenes, have the QC send one CC with values 0, 1, 2… and map that CC to Scene in T3KJ and to the scene switch in dJONt: one press switches all three.

10Automating in REAPER

Faceplate controls and the Scene parameter are REAPER parameters, so they can be drawn as envelopes, no MIDI needed. Blocks themselves aren't parameters (the chain changes from preset to preset), so to turn blocks on and off over a song, put them in scenes and automate the scene.

A scene per section (envelope)

  1. Set up the scenes: A verse, B chorus, C lead, and so on.
  2. In REAPER's FX window for T3KJ: Param → Show track envelope → Scene. (For a faceplate knob, touch it in T3KJ first, then Param → Show track envelope picks the last touched control.)
  3. Set the envelope points to square so it jumps instead of sliding, and draw the steps: each step is a scene (A at the bottom, H at the top).
  4. Leave the track in Read mode. Clicking a scene while playing back follows the envelope again at the next point.

With MIDI instead

  1. Settings → MIDI Mapping → Scene (value 0–7 = A–H), then send the CC once (e.g. CC 20).
  2. Put an empty MIDI item on the same track over the song, open it, add a CC 20 lane and draw one event per section: value 0 = A, 1 = B, 2 = C…
  3. dJONt before T3KJ passes the MIDI on, so the same item can switch dJONt's scenes too (map the same CC there).
Why not automate block power over MIDI? Block power and footswitch targets flip on each press, so automation gets out of step if a press is missed or you start playback mid-song. Scene values can't get out of step.

12Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
REAPER doesn't list T3KJ, or lists TK3JRe-scan VST3. TK3J was the old name; the installer removes it. Use T3KJ (the "T3 over knobs" icon).
No sound in the standalone app☰ → Settings: input device and channels, outputs, and turn off the input mute (the banner says when it's muted).
A scene switch doesn't change somethingPitch and the loaded captures aren't part of scenes. For a different amp per scene, load each amp as its own block and turn them on per scene.
The QC loads the wrong presetPC n = the nth preset in the browser's order (turn on MIDI PC to see the numbers). Match the order in dJONt.
A footswitch is "backwards"Footswitch and block power targets flip per press; stomp it once to resync, or use scenes for anything that must be exact.
Crackles / high CPUBigger buffer, A2-Lite, oversampling off, Multi-Core on. Dual Mono doubles the amp CPU.
Everything feels latePitch Shift adds latency while it's on; turn it off or shorten its Buffer.
Two chains sound thin togetherFlip one chain's Ø, or use Auto Align.
Something elseSettings → Diagnostics → Copy Logs, and paste them to Claude.
